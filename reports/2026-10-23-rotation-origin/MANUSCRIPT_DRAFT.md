# Following the pressure increment to local velocity gradients

On 1071 material pressure pairs shared by three admissible normal layers, the case-midpoint identity separated the Lamb-vector increment into velocity-change and vorticity-change terms. For the 0.012–0.040L layer, their mean paired-load contributions were −0.000284096 and +0.002387490, respectively. The positive net increment was therefore carried by the vorticity-change term in this inertial frame and decomposition, while the velocity-change term partially opposed it. The corresponding body-only sampled control was substantially smaller. This exact algebraic identity does not establish independent causal fractions.

Projecting the vorticity onto a local tangent frame showed that the principal contribution came from the cross-chord tangent component. Here the chord tangent is the global streamwise axis projected onto the instantaneous surface, rather than an anatomically reconstructed chord line. The cross-chord vorticity difference was decomposed exactly into a normal derivative of chordwise velocity and the negative chordwise derivative of normal velocity. Both provided positive paired contributions in the near-side resolved layer. The normal-shear contribution decreased markedly in the more distant layer, whereas the normal-velocity derivative contribution remained positive. These pointwise gradient contractions should not be identified with a measured streamline-curvature term.

The upper resolved layer supplied 0.001846270 of the 0.002103394 net Lamb increment, compared with 0.000257124 from the lower layer. The chordwise derivative of normal velocity was particularly asymmetric, contributing positively on the upper side and slightly negatively on the lower side. These are finite-layer momentum terms, not absolute single-surface pressure loads.

The pressure control showed a complementary distinction. From an offset of 0.040L to 0.012L, the paired B−I pressure projection increased by 0.001183060, while the body-only sampled pressure projection decreased by 0.000358640. The combination therefore did not reproduce the body-only pressure variation near the absent-fin location. Together with the previous moving/static controls, this supports an interaction-induced modification of the near-fin velocity gradients and pressure distribution. Kinetic, unsteady and viscous contributions, the outer pressure and the wall gap must still be retained to connect these local terms to the surface pressure difference; a named-vortex force fraction is not inferred.

All comparisons use the same 1071 pairs, covering 95.97% of the common upper area. The 0.008–0.030L candidate was excluded because the joint all-term, all-phase validity requirement retained only one pair. The results use the existing 17-phase profiles and are not a new CFD calculation or a convergence demonstration. The exact identities were checked numerically, and independent time-dependent pressure offsets changed the paired signals by at most 6.94×10⁻¹⁸.
