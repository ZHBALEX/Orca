# Gauge-invariant pressure differences under body–fin coupling

Internal revision, 23 October 2026. This note supersedes interpretations of absolute single-surface pressure residuals in v11–v12. Numerical values and methods are documented in [the current report](README.md) and [output](pressure_jump.json).

For the Neumann pressure boundary conditions specified for this study, the pressure field admits an additive spatially uniform constant unless a reference is imposed. That constant may differ between simulations and between saved times. Consequently, subtracting the body-only pressure from a coupled-minus-isolated surface pressure does not, in general, yield a gauge-invariant measure of additional suction. We therefore use the paired pressure difference J = (p_lower − p_upper)/(0.5ρU²), and examine its change between the coupled and isolated configurations.

Corresponding material faces were identified in both simulations. Lower-surface pressures were interpolated at the reference x–z coordinates of upper-surface face centers, separately for the two fins. The construction retained 2,169 query faces, representing approximately 98.73% of the common-region upper-surface area, without extrapolation. This is a material-chart comparison and does not assume exact alignment along the instantaneous normal of the deformed finite-thickness fin. Adding distinct time-varying pressure constants to the two simulations changed the coupled-minus-isolated pressure jump by at most 2.22×10⁻¹⁵.

The cycle-averaged, area-weighted pressure jump was 0.41757 in the coupled case and 0.26030 in the isolated case, giving a difference of 0.15727. Over 0.75≤t/T≤1, the increment was 0.45163 near the selected RV+ branch and 0.59573 in an equal-area, non-overlapping upstream control. Thus, the spatially distributed pressure response persists when the diagnostic is made independent of the pressure datum. These quantities are pressure differences, not lift coefficients or an attribution of force to a vortex.

A separate geometric audit showed that the fin-labeled region in the coupled case is open at its junction, whereas the isolated-fin surface is closed. An additive unit Cp would change the coupled fin-region pressure lift coefficient by up to 0.006655, while the corresponding isolated whole-fin integral is invariant. Hence, direct subtraction of those regional pressure forces requires a defined reference or closure convention. The identical common-face region is also open and does not eliminate this ambiguity when each case has its own pressure constant.

The present evidence supports a change in the cross-fin pressure difference over a broader near-root region. It does not establish a unique RV+ load contribution. Completing the link to integrated loading requires an explicit treatment of the junction, side surfaces and the distinction between the marker-pressure integral and the solver's Cartesian-interface load evaluation. Earlier layer-pressure differences and velocity–vorticity identities remain gauge invariant, but the previous percentage comparison based on absolute residual magnitudes is withdrawn.
