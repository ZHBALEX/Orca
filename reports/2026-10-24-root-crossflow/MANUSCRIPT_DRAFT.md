# Results draft: body-associated crossflow and motion-dependent pressure enhancement

The body alters the near-root flow in a specific, measurable direction. On the negative-z fin, where positive Cartesian W denotes motion toward the root, the cycle-mean lower-side velocity at span fraction 0.2 changes from W/U=0.0971 for the isolated fin to −0.0363 for the body–fin configuration. The body-only control independently produces outward and downward velocity disturbances at corresponding absent-fin locations. These observations support a body-associated redirection of the near-root flow, although the combined velocity field cannot be obtained by linearly superposing the body-only and isolated-fin solutions.

The reduction of rootward flow persists across six open-gate definitions. Integrating the positive Cartesian lateral velocity over three spanwise locations and two lower-side clearance intervals gives an 80.7–91.2% reduction of cycle-mean rootward flux in the body–fin configuration. Both cases are evaluated at identical gate coordinates, and all gate points are fluid in both configurations. This quantity measures transport through the selected open gates; it does not measure completed lower-to-upper leakage around the root. Paired three-dimensional particle trajectories do not establish consistent suppression of such complete trajectories, so a root-leakage-blockage interpretation is not adopted.

Frozen-geometry controls distinguish this flow redirection from the larger motion-dependent pressure response. At span fraction 0.2, the mean lower-side lateral-velocity contrast is −0.1333U in the moving cases and −0.1190U in the frozen cases, whereas the corresponding mean lower-minus-upper surface-pressure contrast increases from 0.0283 in the frozen comparison to 0.3291 in the moving comparison. The upper-front negative-vorticity window integral is also larger in the moving comparison. Thus, substantial lateral-flow modification exists without motion, but it is insufficient to account for the pressure enhancement observed with motion. Because the control freezes both body and fin motion, it does not isolate their individual kinematic contributions.

The pressure–force connection is evaluated using within-case lower-minus-upper pressure differences, which are invariant to the arbitrary pressure constant associated with the Neumann formulation. Integration over the common two-fin region gives a positive mean pressure-load contrast of 0.006139, with 84.73% concentrated in the inner 60% of the common span. A separate, explicitly bounded near-wall momentum analysis identifies a positive rotational pressure contribution partly offset by kinetic and unsteady terms. The pressure at the outer boundary of that layer remains a substantial contribution and cannot be omitted. These results support a mechanism interpretation involving body-associated flow redirection and a motion-dependent near-fin response that increases the mean inner-fin pressure difference.

The intervening vorticity-production pathway is not quantitatively resolved by the present postprocessing. An Eulerian vorticity balance evaluated at matched upper-front positions has a substantial residual, and it therefore does not identify a dominant stretching, tilting, or diffusive contribution. The evidence supports the measured flow and pressure contrasts and their mechanical connection to force; it does not establish a unique vortex-mediated causal chain or a percentage of total lift attributable to a named vortex.
