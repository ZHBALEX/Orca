# Mean-flow changes accompanying the mean pressure enhancement

Exploratory draft; human author verification pending.

Phase averages at matched moving-section coordinates revealed persistent changes in the inner-fin flow. At span fraction 0.3, the average upper-side spanwise velocity within the sampled window changed from −0.47892U in the isolated-fin configuration to −0.55969U with the body present. The corresponding mean spanwise-vorticity component changed from −7.1952U/L to −9.7878U/L. Over the same chord and clearance sampling grid, the mean lower-minus-upper fluid pressure coefficient increased by 0.21079. These are averages over off-surface sampling points and must be distinguished from surface-force integrals. [mean_section.json]

An exact product expansion of the local Lamb pressure term separated products of phase-mean velocity and vorticity from their deviations. On the established common paired surface support, the mean–mean contribution was +0.00223684, whereas the deviation–deviation contribution was −0.00014679. The two mixed contributions summed to +0.00001335, recovering the total +0.00210339. The instantaneous geometric projection was retained throughout; hence the mixed terms were not assumed to vanish. [mean_flow_pressure.json, result.terms]

These results support an association between the mean pressure enhancement and altered mean near-fin flow, rather than attributing the mean local rotational pressure contribution to the strongest instantaneous vortex event. The phase averages follow moving sampling coordinates and are not stationary Eulerian mean fields. The origin of the body-induced flow modification and the outer-layer pressure contribution remain unresolved; the decomposition is not a whole-system causal force partition.
