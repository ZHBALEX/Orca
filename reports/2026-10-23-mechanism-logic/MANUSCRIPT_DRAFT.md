# Spatially distributed pressure changes in body–fin coupling

Internal mechanism draft, 23 October 2026. Exploratory results for the present case; not a submission-ready manuscript. Source IDs L01–L08 refer to [the literature matrix](LITERATURE_MATRIX.md). Numerical results refer to [the spatial-control output](spatial_controls.json) unless specified otherwise.

## Motivation and question

Interactions between a body and its propulsive surfaces can modify both vortex evolution and surface loading. Previous studies have examined these effects through controlled comparisons of body and fin configurations (L01–L03). However, a change in an identifiable vortex does not, by itself, determine how much of the force difference is attributable to that structure. Changes in crossflow and in the distribution of pressure can accompany changes in vortex strength (L04–L05). The present analysis therefore asks whether the additional suction observed near the selected positive streamwise-vorticity branch, denoted RV+, is spatially specific to that region.

This question follows from the preceding analysis, which identified a coupling-dependent branch and different streamwise-vorticity histories in paired trajectories. Those observations characterize the selected flow structures; they do not establish a unique vortex-induced force. A second limitation concerns pressure reconstruction: the previous full-upper-surface resolved-layer audit gave a cycle-mean pressure discrepancy of 2.27%, but an error normalized by the RMS fluctuation of the measured signal of 37.94% ([v11 audit](../2026-10-23-evidence-audit/README.md)). Consequently, agreement in the mean cannot be used to establish local or phase-resolved accuracy.

## Comparisons and diagnostic quantities

We compare the coupled body–fin case B, the isolated-fin case I, and a no-fin body case N. The surface diagnostic is R = Cp,B − Cp,I − Cp,N, where the body-only pressure is sampled at the corresponding virtual fin locations. This subtraction removes a sampled incident-pressure control; it is not a unique decomposition of nonlinear interaction forces. Common pressure references remain a prerequisite for interpreting this open-surface diagnostic.

At phase t/T = 0.75, a material patch of radius 0.015L was fixed around the upper-surface point nearest the previously selected RV+ core. Four geometric target offsets, ±0.03L in x and z, defined nearby controls. Surface elements were accumulated by distance, with a fractional final element, to match the reference area exactly at that phase. All masks were subsequently retained by material index. Actual displacements and overlaps are reported because the curved surface does not permit all requested offsets. The reference patch and analysis window were inherited from earlier observations, so the comparisons remain exploratory.

## Results

The late-window mean residual pressure was −0.310 in the RV+ patch. The equal-area upstream control, which did not overlap the reference, had a more negative value of −0.370 (figure 5). Thus, the selected patch did not contain the strongest suction among the examined regions. Excluding all reference elements from the controls did not change the upstream value. Another control retained a value of −0.363 under this exclusion, although its geometric location should not be interpreted as an independent spanwise sample. These comparisons do not exclude a nonlocal pressure influence of RV+; they show that spatial coincidence with the selected core is insufficient to establish specificity.

Here, measured pressure denotes a directly sampled CFD quantity, not an experimental measurement. The normal-momentum balance further distinguished the regions (figure 6). In the reference patch, the measured residual pressure drop over h/L = 0.012–0.040 was −0.16345. The Lamb, kinetic-gradient, unsteady and viscous terms were +0.00616, −0.19566, +0.05226 and −0.00309, respectively, yielding a reconstruction of −0.14033. In the upstream control, the Lamb term was −0.59631 and was partly offset by a kinetic-gradient term of +0.30524 and an unsteady term of +0.10293. Neither region exhibited a negligible closure residual. Replacing the Fourier derivative with a central difference produced reconstructed layer values of −0.14268 and −0.19093 for the reference and upstream patches, respectively, compared with measured values of −0.16345 and −0.23581. Similarity between derivative estimates therefore did not remove the local discrepancy.

To interpret the small reference-patch Lamb sum, we applied the exact midpoint identity Δ(u×ω) = Δu×ω̄ + ū×Δω, where the overbar denotes the midpoint between B and I, not a temporal average. After projection and integration over the same layer, the reference-patch velocity- and vorticity-difference terms were −0.18767 and +0.19723. The body-control term was −0.00340, giving the small positive sum +0.00616 (figure 7). The corresponding upstream terms were +0.26583, −0.86653 and +0.00439. The small reference sum therefore resulted from cancellation of larger terms. This algebraic decomposition does not uniquely allocate causal effects between velocity and vorticity, which are coupled properties of the same flow.

## Interpretation and limits

Taken together, the comparisons are consistent with a distributed reorganization of near-root pressure under body–fin coupling. They do not support the stronger assertion that suction is uniquely concentrated in the selected RV+ patch or that its Lamb contribution measures the branch's force contribution. This distinction matters because pressure-force attribution can depend on both rotation-dominated and strain-dominated regions, as well as their geometric influence weights (L07). A local normal-momentum balance is not equivalent to that volume-based force partition.

The analysis follows the progression from force differences to selected phases, spatially resolved flow quantities and surface pressure used in L05, while retaining a limitation exposed by the present controls. Experimental pressure-force studies also show why amplitude and timing must be validated separately and why three-dimensional flow and shear cannot be ignored without evidence (L08). The present checks establish algebraic and implementation consistency, not independent experimental validation.

A complete mechanism claim will require the pressure difference integrated over the same complete, two-sided fin surface to reproduce the relevant load difference, with pressure-reference, uncovered-area and viscous contributions audited. A subsequent moving-boundary force partition or controlled geometric/kinematic intervention could then test whether the RV+ region has a robust load contribution. Until those tests are available, the branch should be described as a coupling-dependent feature associated with a broader pressure response, rather than the demonstrated cause of the total lift enhancement.
