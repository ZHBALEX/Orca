# Cycle robustness and limits of mechanistic attribution

Exploratory results draft, not a submission-ready manuscript. Human author verification and approval remain pending.

The native whole-system force histories support a positive mean lift interaction, defined as the body–fin load minus the isolated-fin and finless-body loads. Integration over the selected recorded cycles gave a total increment of 0.00589876, consisting of a pressure contribution of 0.00612014 and a viscous contribution of −0.00022124. Combining the last three complete recorded cycles of each configuration yielded 27 cycle selections, all with positive total increments (0.00573243–0.00619176). This range measures sensitivity to the selection of late cycles; the cycles are correlated records and are not independent experimental replicates. [E17: cycle_evidence.json, selected_cycle_mean and late_cycle_cartesian_product]

The force histories also expose a limitation of the pressure-field sampling. Retaining 16 intervals per cycle increased the inferred mean pressure interaction to 0.00672894, approximately 9.95% above integration using all 480 recorded intervals. Increasing the retained sampling density did not produce a monotonic sequence. These calculations vary the quadrature of an existing force history, not the simulation time step, and therefore do not establish temporal discretization convergence. They likewise cannot certify the convergence of local pressure-gradient or acceleration estimates. [E17: pressure_sampling]

Together with the material-paired pressure comparisons and moving/static controls, these results support a motion-associated enhancement of relative pressure loading. The local Lamb-term and velocity-gradient decompositions provide a plausible flow-based account of this enhancement. They do not uniquely identify an individual vortex as the cause of the whole-system lift increment: the local integration domain differs from the system-force domain, and unresolved near-wall and phase-dependent budget contributions remain. A stronger attribution requires authenticated simulation provenance, denser field output, spatial and temporal refinement, and controls that vary body and fin motion separately.
