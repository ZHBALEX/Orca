# Mechanistic interpretation supported by four existing simulations

The body-associated increment in the paired common-fin pressure-load proxy increased from 0.001440 for the stationary geometries to 0.006139 for the moving geometries. Freezing the motion at the mean pose therefore reduced this increment by 76.55%. The stationary surfaces matched the cycle-mean moving surfaces to within approximately 5.4×10⁻⁷ body lengths, and the moving body–fin and isolated-fin cases shared identical common-fin kinematics. This comparison identifies a motion-dependent body–fin response, although freezing the entire geometry does not separately isolate body motion and fin motion.

The increment was primarily a change in the cycle-mean pressure jump rather than a correlation between pressure and surface orientation. The mean-pressure/mean-projection term was 0.00613832, whereas their covariance contributed only 0.00000111. The inner 60% of the common material span accounted for 84.73% of the increment, with the second and fourth quarters contributing 55.53% and 36.10%, respectively. These signed integrals localize the response in space and phase; they are based on 17 field-output phases and are not time-converged fractions.

Upstream probes showed an increase in mean relative speed squared but a decrease in mean incidence inboard. At span fraction 0.2 and 0.30 chord lengths upstream, these changes were +12.34% and −2.67°, respectively. A simple instantaneous relative-speed-squared times sin(2α) proxy also had a negative mean B−I change at the three innermost sampled sections, opposite to the positive pressure-jump change. Thus an increase in mean incidence, or this simple quasi-steady proxy, does not explain the inboard pressure gain. The probes include self-induced flow and should not be treated as independently imposed inflow conditions.

Reintegration of the existing auxiliary-potential results further distinguished direct acceleration from the pressure response associated with rotational flow. The mean acceleration increment was small and negative in all three available domains, whereas the resolved Q-source increment remained positive across two domains and three near-wall exclusions. Its rotational component was positive in all six combinations. These results support rotational pressure-source contributions to the motion-dependent mean response; they do not equate a named vortex with the complete lift increment. The potential target uses a different one-fin region and reference convention from the paired proxy, and a negative unassigned term remains, so their numerical fractions must not be combined.

Together, the controls support a motion-dependent, predominantly inboard pressure enhancement associated with body–fin interaction. The body alters the flow response of the moving fin, increases the mean lower-to-upper pressure difference over the inner common surface, and thereby increases the mean regional pressure load. Resolving the remaining source terms is necessary for a unique force allocation to RV+ or any other individual vortex, but is not required to distinguish this observed response from a purely static-body, mean-incidence, geometric-projection, or direct mean-acceleration explanation.
