# Results draft — pressure differences, loads and bounded attribution

For the stored cycle, the closed-body contrast B−N−I yielded mean pressure, viscous and total lift coefficients of 0.0061201, -0.0002212 and 0.0058987, respectively. The pressure contrast remained positive after the viscous contribution was included. These values use 481 force samples per case; using only the 17 field-output phases increased the mean pressure contrast by 9.95%. This comparison characterizes the three stored simulations. Shared-vertex motion was checked, but complete surface-kinematic equivalence and multi-cycle convergence have not been established.

To remove the independent pressure constants permitted by the Neumann formulation, we compared paired lower-to-upper pressure differences on the common material surface. Their integrated lift proxy had a mean B−I increment of 0.0061394. With an explicit lower-surface pressure reference, the corresponding Cartesian-interface regional load increment was 0.0063437. The paired proxy differed from that regional signal by 8.96% of its fluctuation standard deviation; including the finite-geometry, unpaired-surface and interpolation remainder reduced the marker-to-interface discrepancy to 1.03%. In contrast, comparison against the whole-system signal produced a 75.1% normalized RMS difference despite a correlation of 0.9926. Agreement of the cycle averages therefore does not establish that the common-fin pressure increment accounts for the whole-system response.

For one fin, the jointly valid pressure pairs covered 96.24% of the common upper-surface area. The paired surface-pressure increment was separated exactly into an outer-pressure term, a resolved normal-layer difference and a wall-gap term. Their mean lift-proxy contributions were 0.0018711, 0.0011841 and 0.0000402. This is spatial pressure bookkeeping, not a causal partition. Within the resolved layer, the positive Lamb contribution was opposed by kinetic-gradient and unsteady terms. The reconstructed mean was within 3.18% of the measured mean, while the phase-resolved error remained 39.2% of the measured fluctuation standard deviation and increased to 61.2% with a central time derivative. These results support a balance among competing terms, rather than identification of the Lamb term with a vortex-specific lift contribution.

Previously computed auxiliary-potential results passed the algebraic target checks but retained an unassigned regional mean equal to −55.6% of their original target. That target and pressure convention differ from the present closed-system contrast. Only 44 of 98 stored vorticity-transport windows had valid samples for all terms; their median absolute residual relative to the vorticity change was 47.7%. Together with the stronger paired-pressure increment in an equal-area upstream control than near RV+, these findings support distributed pressure redistribution associated with body–fin interaction, but do not establish a unique RV+ force fraction or a quantitatively closed origin of the streamwise-vorticity sign change.
