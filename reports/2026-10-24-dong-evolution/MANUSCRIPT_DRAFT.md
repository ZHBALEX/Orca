# Phase evolution separates pressure peaks from later rotational enhancement

Exploratory results draft; human author verification remains pending.

Extending the matched-section comparison to all saved phases altered the interpretation of the peak-phase observations. The largest inner-fin pressure-jump increments occurred at t/T=0 and 0.5, whereas the additional negative spanwise-vorticity integral in the upper-front fluid window peaked at t/T=0.6875–0.75. Restricting the integral to Q-positive cells placed all three inner-station maxima at t/T=0.75. The spatial coexistence of increased vorticity and pressure jump at a selected phase therefore does not establish synchronous development throughout the cycle. [evolution.json: summary]

The pressure-jump increment was strongly anticorrelated with the recorded local vertical acceleration (r=−0.958, −0.955 and −0.942 at span fractions 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4). These descriptive relationships support an acceleration-associated fluctuating pressure response. They do not identify an added-mass coefficient or explain the positive cycle mean: the fitted intercept is unconstrained, and the phases are not independent samples. [evolution.json: motion_correlations and descriptive_acceleration_fits]

Backward fluid trajectories were then initialized in the enhanced upper-front flow at t/T=0.75. For the body–fin configuration, all 21 perturbed release trajectories stopped within 0.006L of the fin, with none associated with the body under this distance criterion. The corresponding isolated-fin trajectories included 15 near-fin endpoints and six more distant stops. These paths support a near-fin association for the sampled fluid, without demonstrating its vorticity-generation mechanism or a coherent-vortex identity. Thus direct transport of a body vortex into this sampled region has not been established; body-induced modification of the fin-local flow remains the mechanism to discriminate.
